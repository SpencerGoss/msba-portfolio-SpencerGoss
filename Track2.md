# Track 2: Implementation Strategy

## What would be implemented:

* Our team recommends implementing an **Early-Lifecycle Commitment Program** that targets customers at the highest churn risk—specifically newly tenured customers on month-to-month contracts. The program would offer incentives such as price locks or free trials to streaming or add-on services in exchange for converting to longer-term contracts. This targets customers during a critical period when they're most likely to churn while providing tangible value to encourage commitment.


**Success Metric:**
* This implementation would be considered a success if the Early-Lifecycle Commitment Program reduces overall customer churn below 26.58%, with a target of at least a 10-15% relative reduction in churn rate among newly tenured, month-to-month customers within the first six months of program launch. With this addition of the program we would like to reduce the number of month-to-month contracts and lock them into longer term contracts increase their tenure. 



## How would you measure impact and what are two risks or unintended consequences?

**Measurement Approach:**
* Track churn rate monthly for the target segment (newly tenured, month-to-month customers) before and after program launch
* Compare actual churn reduction against the 10-15% relative reduction target
* Monitor conversion rates from month-to-month to longer-term contracts
* Calculate program ROI by dividing incremental revenue from converted customers against total incentive costs

**Risk 1:** Overaggressive conversion tactics could backfire by alienating customers and accelerating churn. Pressuring month-to-month customers to commit to longer contracts before they're ready may increase frustration and drive them to competing services offering greater flexibility. Additionally, if incentives are perceived as manipulative rather than genuinely valuable, they could damage brand trust and customer satisfaction scores.

**Risk 2:** Some incentives would go to customers who would not have churned in the first place, representing wasted marketing spend. This would reduce ROI and increase customer acquisition costs without improving net retention. More sophisticated segmentation and predictive modeling would be needed to identify only high-churn-risk customers and avoid overcasting the incentive net too wide.
